## IMMERSIVE_SCENE_PLAN

- scenePlan: the main scenes in order, each with source project_asset (only DATA.visualAssets.projectClips, real footage), generated (AI still or live-action Veo clip), info_clip (Flow animated 3D infographic explanation) or graphic (brief text/number emphasis) and why. DATA.visualAssets lists what real material exists; do not plan close-ups of real packaging, label text or certification marks as proof when no real product photo exists — generated packaging is not the real label. When DATA.infoClipsAllowed is true, causes, criteria, mechanism, function and the reason our product works require source info_clip: design 1–3 immersive 3D explanation clips, each up to 8 seconds, with visible spatial separation, cutaway, ingredient structure or process motion and a camera move that reveals the explanation. A flat checklist, one static image with arrows or abstract glowing decoration cannot replace this explanation. Ingredient composition is shown as composition, not an invented biological effect. Use generated live action for the concrete customer situation. This is a planning constraint only: never put disclaimers, "concept art" or "not the real package" labels, source IDs or notes about generated imagery into narration, captions or graphics.

## IMMERSIVE_VISUAL_CONTRACT

VISUAL CONTRACT: Start with ONE concrete recognizable event, not two questions that repeat the same disappointment. Every key claim must have a visible corresponding object, relation or change at that moment: a blank or unreadable phone cannot show discovery or ingredient comparison. Never assume generated screen/label text will be readable; choose a visible physical event or the explicit INFO explanation instead. The proof scene must show the current product fact, not replay a hook card with unrelated text. Maintain ivory backgrounds, dark olive typography/graphics and ONE warm-gold accent across photography, INFO, cards and ending. End on the real supplied product name, its core supported ingredient/feature and one next action; use an honest supplied ad card or screen text, never fabricate branded packaging.

## EXPLANATION_PLANNING_RULES

EXPLANATION PLAN: each scenePlan item includes explanation, null for a scene that does not explain structure, composition, comparison or a process. For every info_clip, supply one structured explanation instead of only describing a visual style:
- id: a stable lowercase production identifier, reused unchanged when this scene becomes an INFO clip.
- productForm: the supplied product's recognizable real form and scale cues. Preserve that form before showing its inside. A capsule product stays recognizable as a capsule; it is not a machine chamber, a glass storage tank or an unexplained giant bead. Other product categories retain their own form. Unknown branding does not erase the known physical form. Use a schematic only for the part or relationship that needs simplification, and say what it represents in the plan.
- entities: stable id, name, representation (product, component or schematic), appearance. Declare each relevant product, part or ingredient once; keep its identity, material, color and meaning through every beat. Distinguish outer shell from contents when the claim refers only to contents. Do not invent an ingredient, quantity, mixture ratio, anatomical process or health outcome.
- beats: stable id, targetIds, before, action, after, narrationCue, viewerTakeaway, startProgress and endProgress. Each beat makes ONE relationship understandable by showing a visible change: a surface becomes transparent, a real part is cut away, a path reveals where something goes, or an aligned comparison exposes a supported difference. Write what was hidden before, what moves/changes, and what the viewer can now see. startProgress/endProgress are ordered, nonoverlapping proportions from 0 to 1 of the eventual eight-second clip, including enough time to perceive the result. They are planned action timing, not measured footage. narrationCue is a phrase in the accompanying spoken copy, not a new claim. Use the available time to explain, not to add decorative motion.
- annotations: targetId, beatId, label, kind (label, pointer, direction or measure), motionIntent. The target must participate in the referenced beat. A pointer identifies one exact component; a directional arrow explains the described motion; a measurement mark explains a supported dimension. motionIntent states which target point it follows and when it appears or leaves. Compose these arrows, leader lines, measurement marks and exact labels into the complete INFO image before animating it. Keep the CLEAN source recognizable and the INFO layout readable. Motion follows the named target and preserves completed label shapes; these are production intentions, not measured tracking coordinates. Keep diagram labels distinct from spoken subtitles and keep both readable.
Comparison uses matching viewpoint and scale, with stable identities on each side. If the issue is ingredient composition, show which ingredients make each composition rather than one container versus two containers. Do not imply a mixed oil physically separates into layers or balls unless that behavior is established by the supplied material. A cutaway shows an interior; it does not eject a new object just to create motion.
Connect explanation to the actual discovery: expectation → visible discrepancy → understandable reason or comparison → the identified product. If the discovery depends on text, designate the exact supplied wording and a readable app-composited presentation; an empty phone is never evidence. Natural copy states what the viewer sees at that moment, without asking the same disappointment twice. One ivory/olive/warm-gold visual system continues across the scene and ending.

## EXPLANATION_SCRIPT_RULES

STRUCTURED EXPLANATION DELIVERY:
- Copy the corresponding DATA.planning.concept.scenePlan[].explanation into each infoClip.explanation, preserving its stable id, product form, entity IDs, beat IDs, relationships and viewer takeaways. Write null only for clips from older planning with no explanation contract. Clean/info prompts, graphicOrder and early/mid/late camera plan must describe that same contract, not redesign its objects. The CLEAN frame establishes the recognizable product and the first beat's before state; INFO is the final beat's after state with its finished target-linked arrows, leader lines, measurement marks and exact annotation labels. Reveal structure by transparency or a justified cutaway, not arbitrary machine doors or unsupported loose objects.
- Use each beat's normalized startProgress/endProgress as its position in the original eight-second source. Reserve enough contiguous footage for the action, its narration and a short readable result; no forced fast cuts or default silent eight-second holds. Use the complete clip or ordered source ranges covering the required beats. Fit camera movement around the explanation so that labels remain readable and the same target remains identifiable.
- Each sentence includes actionSync: null for an unbound sentence, otherwise {clipId, beatId} for the explanation beat named by that sentence. Its cuts must show that clip and the bound beat. narrationCue must be an exact phrase present in that sentence; align that spoken phrase with the beat's visible change. If copy polishing changes wording, update narrationCue to the new wording while retaining the meaning and IDs. Never bind a sentence to an unrelated early phase just because the cut begins there. The app refines timing from measured speech; the plan must not claim the generated action was measured.
- Keep explanation.annotations tied to their targetId and beatId. Build the COMPLETE INFO image with these exact labels, pointers, directional arrows and measurement marks already composed beside their named objects. Flow then animates CLEAN to that supplied infographic; labels appear as completed shapes at the relevant beat and remain attached to the same target. Do not invent or retype new words during motion. The app adds spoken subtitles separately; it must not duplicate the baked diagram labels with sentence callouts. Prefer callouts [] for that explanation sentence. Additional nonduplicate emphasis may use a callout with targetId naming the exact entity, or null for unrelated emphasis. These directions are a production plan, not proof that generated footage was measured or that tracking succeeded. A right-hand label must name the exact target, not accidentally both items on that side.
- A composition explanation may show shell and contents separately, but proportions or purity refer only to the supported scope. Never substitute a shiny oil orb for the capsule product or portray an unsupported effect inside a human body. Restore the product's recognizable exterior or connect to the supplied product asset at the end. The final product name, one supported feature and next action stay legible together.

## IMMERSIVE_SCRIPT_RULES

IMMERSIVE PRODUCTION CONTRACT (new planning policy):
- Total may exceed 35 seconds and use up to 60. Choose time from complete thoughts, natural speech and readable action. Allow roughly 6–8 Korean characters including spaces per second of actual speech plus short visual pauses; this is an estimate, not a quota. Do not plan a full 60 seconds of nonstop speech. Never accelerate narration or trim off an animated result to meet an arbitrary target.
- Open with ONE concrete event in the chosen viewer's situation, not two questions repeating the same pain. Make the event visible. A blank/unreadable phone is not discovery, comparison or proof. Without readable source material, use a visible object/action or explicit INFO comparison; do not pretend a blank screen contains evidence.
- In Flow mode, real_cause, requirement/criteria, mechanism/function and reason_why sentences MUST include a used infoClip I1..I3. scenePlan source info_clip must survive into the storyboard. Use 1–3 clips that animate a meaningful spatial relation: parts separate, a cutaway reveals contents, ingredient groups move into a supported composition, or a flow shows an evidenced process. Plan beginning, transformation and understood result in one coherent 3D space. Decorative arrows/rings on an unchanged photograph, a text checklist or static card cannot replace this explanation. A composition fact does not license an invented bodily mechanism.
- Preserve animation build-up as well as its result. Use early → mid → late chronologically, or one full early-phase shot, rather than only the finished INFO frame. One INFO clip supplies 8 seconds total. A continuous explanation may hold up to 8 seconds; split only at a meaningful visible change. Match graphicOrder, camera plan, cut goal and narration.
- Product proof shows the current fact. At the first spoken product name, show that same supplied name with a readable screen label or callout; an anonymous model alone does not identify the product. A hook ad-card headline cannot illustrate a new ingredient/origin claim. Use supported INFO visuals or supplied product assets; an approved image is an honest ad card and belongs to its matching reveal/ending.
- One art direction: ivory background, dark olive type/graphics, ONE warm-gold accent. Use the same palette in styleAnchor and every image/INFO prompt. No unrelated black/yellow slides or cyan/pink neon. Avoid rings/checks/arrows over dense labels and duplicate text; one readable label naming the actual subject is enough.
- Natural copy: replace 혼합 여부를 읽다 with a concrete comparison. State purity/ingredient facts once and develop their meaning. A necessary scope qualifier belongs with its claim in narration or relevant callout, not in a repeated separate claim. Leave disclaimer empty unless additional necessary disclosure remains; never invent or duplicate permanent scope text.
- End with the supplied product name, ONE core supported ingredient/feature and ONE next action visibly connected. Use actual supplied assets or legible screen text; never invent branded packaging. A generic person looking away with only 비교해보세요 is not a product ending.
