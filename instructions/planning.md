## PLANNING_TARGET_AND_SOLUTION

TARGET AND SOLUTION: when DATA.target exists, the viewer IS that target — build audience from its fragments (situation, desire, pain, failed attempt, believed cause, hesitation) and reuse their quote wording; never switch to another person. When DATA.hypothesis.solutionPath exists, the video's development walks its steps in order and payoff is its outcome: for cause_reframe, show the past attempt, why they think it failed, the real cause, what to check instead, how our product meets it and how the viewer can verify it; for criteria_first, open the target's moment, give the criteria before choosing, then mechanism and verification. ## PLANNING_DECISIONS

Also decide, before any copy:
- viewerChange: what is better in the viewer's own situation after watching (a decision made easier, a worry removed, a routine that now works). - mutedMessage: one Korean line (≤40 chars) that a viewer gets with the sound OFF, from what is shown and written on screen. The screen copy must carry it.
- stopReason: what the viewer SEES in the first 2 seconds that makes them stop scrolling (a visible action, contrast, problem moment or surprising object) — not a slogan, not a calm establishing shot.

## SOURCE_CREATIVE_PLAN

Start from the customer, not from ad count or campaign settings. STEP 0 fragments and targets: before anything else, extract as MANY scene fragments as the data supports (up to 40) from VOICES (origin customer_review), reference copy/analyses (origin reference_ad) and mediaAnalysesByReference transcripts and cuts (origin reference_video); add origin inferred fragments only when the data is thin, with empty sourceIds. Each fragment is one person-moment: situation (when/where), desire (the state they want), pain (what blocks them now), alternative (what they use or do now), failedAttempt (something they already tried that did not work, empty if none seen), believedCause (why THEY think it failed), hesitation (price, doubt or other reason they do not buy yet), quote (the original wording when available) and sourceIds. Use referenceGroups: each analysed reference represents a group of copy variants; a high variantCount means the competitor keeps pushing that message (a strong signal of what customers respond to), while a moment no group addresses is a gap worth a target. Mine competitor ads for the customer moments and hesitations they address (e.g. repeated doubts about authenticity, freshness or price); never carry a competitor's product claim into our facts. Then group similar fragments into targets (id t1.., label = situation + desire + pain in one line, experience tried_failed when the fragments show a failed attempt, otherwise first_time, fragmentIds). Prefer targets that are repeated across sources and include at least one that references do not already address when the data shows one. Score every target with fit: painStrength (1–5, how sharp and frequent the pain is in the customers' own words), productAnswer (1–5, how DIRECTLY and UNIQUELY our FACTS resolve that pain's real cause; a pain any product in the category answers equally scores 1–2), distinctness (1–5, how different the resulting message is from the supplied references), and a one-line reason. The concepts below must use the highest-scoring targets (score = product of the three). STEP 1 customerQuestions: map why customers buy (buying_reason), why they hesitate (hesitation), and what they must confirm before deciding (decision_criterion). Phrase each question in the customer's own words where VOICES supply them. basis customer_voice requires VOICES sourceIds; product_fact requires FACTS sourceIds; otherwise basis inferred with empty sourceIds (an unverified hypothesis). proofNeeded names what the customer must see to be persuaded. answeredByReferences lists supplied reference sourceIds whose observed structure already answers that question, so repeatedly answered questions are visible. STEP 2 design exactly {{imageCount}} distinct square image ad concepts; each concept answers one customerQuestionId and speaks to exactly ONE target (targetId). Output EXACTLY {{imageCount}} hypotheses even when you found more targets; the extra targets stay only in targets. Use different targets for different concepts; only when there are fewer targets than concepts, reuse a target with a different entryPoint. entryPoint is what the ad puts first: pain (problem empathy), alternative (comparison with what they do now), doubt (quality proof), price (value or offer), desire (the outcome state). solutionPath explains HOW our product resolves that target's pain as a process, not a one-line result: for a tried_failed target use flow cause_reframe with stages past_attempt (what they tried) -> believed_cause (why they think it failed) -> real_cause (the actual cause, reframed) -> criteria (what to check instead) -> mechanism (how our product meets it) -> verification (what the viewer can check themselves) -> outcome (the state they get); for a first_time target use flow criteria_first with criteria -> mechanism -> verification -> outcome (optionally real_cause before criteria). Keep stages in that order. Each step has content, basis and sourceIds: mechanism and verification MUST be basis product_fact citing FACTS; real_cause and criteria may use general_knowledge (general category facts, never presented as our product's result) or reference/customer_voice; when FACTS do not show the product category is a registered functional food with an approved function, the reframe is about quality, authenticity or ease, never about health effects. The concept's mechanism signal, message and hook must follow this solutionPath. PERSUASION CHAIN (chain, required, one per concept, the single message of that video): pain (① the customer's pain in their own words, basis customer_voice or reference with sourceIds) -> believedCause (② why they think it happens; content "" when the data shows none) -> realCause (③ the reframed actual cause; general_knowledge or product_fact; never a health effect unless FACTS show a registered functional food) -> requirement (④ what a solution must do to remove ③, written as a condition) -> productFact (⑤ the FACTS statement showing our product meets ④, basis product_fact with sourceIds) -> reasonWhy (⑥ WHY ⑤ is possible: the deeper FACTS behind it, e.g. what one capsule contains and how much; it must add a product detail that ⑤ does not already state — a number, amount, ingredient purity, process or manufacturer from FACTS; restating ⑤ is rejected; basis product_fact with sourceIds) -> outcome (the pain gone and the fragment's desire fulfilled, reusing the customer's own desire words; no invented emotions). Then set exclusivity: product_specific only if ④→⑤→⑥ cannot be said of any other product in the category; if it can, write any_product and choose another target or dig ⑥ deeper. "하루 한 알" alone is any_product; "한 알에 엑스트라 버진 100% 600mg 를 담아서 하루 한 알" is product_specific. Facts that are not part of this chain do NOT belong in this concept's message, copy or script; they belong to other targets. decisionRole follows the fragments: when the target's fragments show a failed attempt, a current alternative or a believed cause, the customer already knows their problem, so decisionRole is comparison (MOFU) or final_decision, never need_awareness; need_awareness only when the pain is still unnamed by the customer. experience is tried_failed whenever any fragment has a failedAttempt. Different concepts must answer different customer questions with different persuasion reasons and proof; changing presenter, background, first line, caption design or format while repeating the same promise is NOT diversity. Prefer questions not already answered by references. Only after the reasons differ, vary hooks and formats. proofShown states which cited FACTS the ad shows as proof. decisionRole marks whether the ad creates need (need_awareness), helps compare options (comparison) or supports the final purchase decision (final_decision); roles are hypotheses to verify with actual business results. The Creative must carry the signals: TOFU primaryText opens with the avatarCallout, then the painPoint, then the mechanism, CTA LEARN_MORE; MOFU leads with the mechanism or reframed cause and its visual proof; BOFU names the brand and states the cited offer, CTA SHOP_NOW. imagePrompt follows the format (offer/guarantee text treatment for BOFU formats). cardSlides: only for mechanism_explainer, write 2-4 follow-up card-news slides after the cover (creative.imagePrompt is the cover card); each slide has a short Korean headline, an optional body line and its own square imagePrompt that renders exactly that headline/body as large legible Korean text on a clean card layout, one step of the mechanism or reframed cause per slide, ending with the proof or the next step; every slide claim must be covered by the concept's claimCitations; all other formats use an empty cardSlides array. Rotate the angles problem_solution, usage_context, objection_answer; use each angle at least once when producing three or more. Reverse-plan each concept from our verified product facts and the observed messages, hooks and edit structures of supplied reference ads. If no reference is supplied, say so; always mark inferred needs as unverified. Distinguish creative-message audiences, not separate Meta ad-set targeting. Each concept also needs genuinely distinct problem, message, hook, visualMechanism and difference. Cosmetic copy/color changes are not diversity. Use mediaAnalysesByReference to borrow reference ad structure: first-screen hook, cut-by-cut screen composition, visible text, and message progression. Do not copy a reference ad's brand claims.
