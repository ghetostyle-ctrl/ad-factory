# hybrid.md — 혼합형(hybrid_explainer_v1) 기획·대본·검토 규칙

고통·상황·결과·행동은 실사, 메커니즘·기능·비교는 3D 설명 세계. `EXPLAINER_GRAMMAR`(R1~R9)는 `{{@EXPLAINER_GRAMMAR}}` 로 다른 절에 끼워진다.
`HYBRID_SCRIPT_RULES` 끝의 `{{hybridExample}}` 은 examples.md 의 HYBRID_EXPLAINER_EXAMPLE 을 코드가 JSON 으로 채운다.
실사 클립 꼬리(`HYBRID_LIVE_TAIL`)는 Flow·Veo 글과 함께 flow.md 에 있다.

## HYBRID_SCENE_PLAN

- scenePlan: the main scenes in order (2–12). Each has source project_asset (only DATA.visualAssets.projectClips, real footage), generated (an AI still or live-action Veo clip of the chosen person's situation) or info_clip (an explainer-world 3D scene; only when DATA.infoClipsAllowed is true, 1–3 of them), a reason, and explainerScene (filled for info_clip, null otherwise; fields in HYBRID SCENE PLAN below). Pain, situation, believed cause, outcome and the next action are live scenes; the real cause, the requirement, the product fact and the reason it works may be explainer scenes (process, comparison or analogy) or live scenes. There is no graphic source: lists and questions are spoken and captioned; a number is shown as an infographic label inside an explainer scene (FACTS only) or spoken and captioned over a live scene. DATA.visualAssets lists what real material exists; do not plan close-ups of real packaging, label text or certification marks as proof when no real product photo exists — generated packaging is not the real label. This is a planning constraint only: never put disclaimers, "concept art" or "not the real package" labels, source IDs or notes about generated imagery into narration, captions or graphics.

## HYBRID_VISUAL_CONTRACT

VISUAL CONTRACT: Start with ONE concrete recognizable event in the live world, not two questions that repeat the same disappointment. Every key claim must have a visible corresponding object, relation or change at that moment: in a live scene a person's visible action with the product in hand (entrance hall, breakfast table, office desk); in an explainer scene an object doing something (opening, filling, rising, standing beside its comparison). In a live scene never rely on generated screen or label text. In an explainer scene the numbers and short Korean labels are drawn into the picture as an infographic — bold arrows, dimension lines, rings and labels attached to the objects, like a 3D architecture-explainer short — using only numbers and facts that exist in FACTS; the app also captions the narration. Two art directions, kept apart: LIVE scenes use real people, concrete places, natural light and the real product form, and their prompts never say "no people", "no products", "no packaging" or "no photographs"; EXPLAINER scenes use clay-white 3D models, two base brand accent colors, explanatory graphics with readable contrast and soft daylight, with no people; the only text is the planned Korean infographic labels. The proof scene must show the current product fact. End on the real supplied product name in a live scene with the product in the person's hand (or the honest supplied ad card for at most {{HYBRID_ENDING_IMAGE_MAX_SEC}} seconds) and one next action; never fabricate branded packaging and never end on a text card.

## EXPLAINER_GRAMMAR

EXPLAINER WORLD GRAMMAR (reference: 3D architecture-explainer shorts; applies to every explainer scene I1..I3):
R1 Every explainer cut is a 3D scene with physical objects, never a text-only panel. First identify the structure, difference, flow or scope the sentence must explain; choose the objects and their relation to show it. Do not repeat the same opening, rotation or pouring action for different explanations and change only the labels. Use only relations supported by FACTS: motion or a diagram is not proof of an unsupported ingredient, origin, ratio or effect.
R2 Text inside the scene is an infographic layer: short Korean labels and numbers (infoLines, 1–4 per scene, each at most 24 characters, only numbers and facts from FACTS) attached to their exact targets. Reveal graphics when the relevant target or action becomes visible: emphasize the target, then show any needed direction or boundary, then the complete label or value. Skip unnecessary stages and decorations; never make all scenes reveal their graphics at the same second. Lines and paths may draw or progress, but Korean labels and numbers appear as complete unchanged units. Keep each graphic attached to the same target through movement and finish in the INFO image's exact position, wording and shape. No speech bubbles, tables, checklists, badges, logos or floating panels; the app adds the spoken caption below.
R3 The existing emphasis kinds remain color_code (object colors distinguish subjects and stay consistent), outline (a clearly visible boundary), glow_line (a line or arrow following an object's surface or travel path) and ghost_object (a translucent physical analogy). Choose line color and thickness for contrast against the object and background; outlines need not be thin or red and glow lines need not be white. Use glow, neon or rings only when they explain the relation, never as free-floating spirals or decoration. Do not obscure key structures or emphasize every element equally.
R4 Use the appropriate physical explanation: exterior to interior for hidden structure, a matched comparison for differences, a tracked path for flow, or a clearly bounded whole for a proportion. Plan real object motion together with the camera; an orbit alone does not explain a new fact. Keep one coherent before/action/after sequence: cleanPrompt is the starting state, actions and plan describe the same ordered changes, and infoPrompt is their final state. Never require an open object in INFO while the last action closes it, or give a label two different appearance times.
R5 A comparison is two models side by side under the same camera, never a table or checklist. They start identical in shape, size and neutral clay-white, then reveal the supported internal difference by a cutaway, translucent shell or cross-section, followed by color coding. Our model keeps one fixed marker (accent1 or a contrasting outline) from its first appearance to the end; the other stays neutral or accent2 and the sides never swap.
R6 An analogy makes a supported quantity or relation tangible; a ratio can use a fill level or equal units with an explicit whole. Distinguish the product's exterior from its contents when a percentage applies only to the contents. Do not invent a physical equivalence, measurement or comparison that FACTS does not support.
R7 Camera movement follows the order of understanding. Describe its start position, target, route through real 3D depth and final viewpoint, not just "orbit" or "rise". Design two or three connected viewpoints across the eight-second source clip with parallax and no cuts; each planned phase still has a camera and action description. Use fast approaches or retreats selectively, not in adjacent scenes with the same pattern. Reduce movement while a label is read, and end in the supplied INFO framing. A cut lasts 2–{{EXPLAINER_CUT_MAX_SEC}} seconds; captions are phrase-sized and last 1–2 seconds.
R8 Keep clay-white models under soft daylight on a warm ivory floor and backdrop, exposure one stop below pure white. Name two base brand accent colors (deep olive green and warm gold when unverified); object colors keep their accent1|accent2|neutral identities. Additional contrasting colors are allowed for explanatory graphics when needed for legibility. The same target and meaning keep the same colors across scenes; distinguish different information clearly. Make the key label, number, arrow or boundary large and bold enough for a phone, with a clear hierarchy, without covering the structure or other labels.
R9 The eight-second clip is source material, not a quota to use up. Select the phases that explain the sentence; each cut uses one phase, ends at a meaningful change or completed action, and holds at most {{EXPLAINER_HOLD_SEC}} second after its last action. If a selected view needs context, include the needed preceding or following phase as an adjacent cut within the existing limits. Plan enough time to read labels within those cuts. Use the single phase timetable {{PHASE_TABLE}}; camera and action text must not repeat or invent numeric time ranges. Adjust the cut arrangement to the narration, never shorten approved copy or add silence to consume unused footage; silence between sentences stays under {{SILENCE_GAP_MAX_SEC}} seconds.

## HYBRID_PLANNING_RULES

HYBRID SCENE PLAN (policy hybrid_explainer_v1): every scenePlan item is either LIVE ACTION or EXPLAINER WORLD, chosen by its source.
- LIVE (source generated or project_asset): the pain, the situation, the believed cause, the outcome and the next action of the persuasion chain. One concrete person in one concrete place (entrance hall, breakfast table, office desk, pharmacy aisle) doing something visible, with the product in their hand or on the table whenever the product is mentioned. Natural light, documentary feel. The opening scene is one visible facial reaction plus one action (looking up from the bottle, putting it down, turning it over) within its first 1.5 seconds; in the pain and believed-cause scenes the person's face stays in frame. Any phone, tablet or monitor in a live scene is switched off (dark glass) so no fake screen text appears. explainerScene is null.
- EXPLAINER (source info_clip, 1–{{INFO_CLIPS_MAX}} items, only when DATA.infoClipsAllowed is true): when DATA.infoClipsAllowed is true, plan at least one explainer scene and prefer two — one comparison for the real cause (two look-alike products that start identical and are told apart by color only once the inside is revealed) and one process for the product fact and the reason it works (what happens inside the product). The real cause, the requirement or criteria, the product fact and the reason it works use it when the supported structure, difference, flow or scope becomes clearer through objects and their relations. Fill explainerScene: sceneType process|comparison|analogy; objects 1–{{EXPLAINER_OBJECTS_MAX}} {subjectId (lowercase letters/digits, 1–12 chars; the storyboard reuses it as a subject id), appearance (one English line: what the object is, its material and shape — the object itself, never a label), color accent1|accent2|neutral}; actions 1–{{EXPLAINER_ACTIONS_MAX}} (English, in order: what the objects physically do); emphasis 0–{{EXPLAINER_EMPHASIS_MAX}} {kind color_code|outline|glow_line|ghost_object, target = one of the objects' subjectId, afterAction = 0-based index of the action it follows}; analogy (English; only for an analogy scene, otherwise ""). The same subjectId keeps the same color in every explainer scene. A comparison has two or more objects in different colors plus a color_code emphasis; its first action keeps them identical from the outside, the reveal (cutaway, translucent shell, cross-section) comes next and the color_code emphasis follows the reveal; the product's own object keeps one fixed marker (accent1 or outline) in every scene. A process is the order of the actions. An analogy names the tangible stand-in for the number.
- There is no graphic source in this policy: a list or a question is spoken and captioned, never shown as a text card; a number lives in an explainer scene as an infographic label (and is spoken) or is spoken and captioned over a live scene.
- The real cause and the requirement are explained by motion: an explainer scene or a live Veo clip. A still image under these two steps is a single cut of at most {{STILL_BEAT_MAX_SEC}} seconds, never two stills in a row (rejected).
- The first scene and the last scene are live action. The last 2–3 seconds show the product in the person's hand (or the honest supplied ad image for at most {{HYBRID_ENDING_IMAGE_MAX_SEC}} seconds); never a text panel, never an explainer scene.
- Two art directions, kept apart. LIVE: real people, concrete places, natural light, the real product form; never write "no people", "no products", "no packaging", "no photographs" or any similar ban for a live scene. If the real product appearance is not supplied, show the product as a plain unbranded container of the known form (for example a white capsule bottle) in the person's hand without inventing a label; never postpone a scene or mark it "on hold" — every scene must be producible now. EXPLAINER: clay-white 3D models, two brand accent colors (name them once, from the brand or packaging; deep olive green and warm gold when unverified), graphics with sufficient contrast, soft daylight, no people; text only as the Korean infographic labels (numbers and facts from FACTS) that the storyboard fixes per scene.

## HYBRID_SCRIPT_RULES

HYBRID PRODUCTION CONTRACT (policy hybrid_explainer_v1):
- BEAT → SOURCE (by chainStep): pain, believed_cause, outcome, cta → live action only (veo_clip A..D or still_image showing the person and the situation). real_cause, requirement, product_fact, reason_why → an explainer clip (veo_clip I1..I3) or live action. Under real_cause and requirement a still_image is at most one cut of {{STILL_BEAT_MAX_SEC}} seconds and never two stills in a row (both rejected); the rest of those sentences plays over explainer cuts or live veo_clip motion. bridge → either. No motion_graphic cut anywhere (rejected). The first cut and the last cut are live action (rejected otherwise). Explainer cuts in total stay under {{EXPLAINER_MAX_PERCENT}}% of the video.
- TWO ANCHORS: styleAnchor describes the live world (the person, the place, clothes, light, the real product form; phones, tablets and monitors switched off, dark glass) and must never contain "no people", "no products", "no packaging", "no photographs" or a similar ban (rejected). explainerAnchor describes the explainer world: clay-white 3D models, the two base brand accent colors (name them) and any needed contrasting graphic colors, soft daylight on a warm ivory floor and backdrop (exposure one stop below pure white), no people, no text except the planned Korean infographic labels. Live prompts (veoClips[].startImagePrompt, stills[].prompt) follow styleAnchor; infoClips[].cleanPrompt and infoPrompt follow explainerAnchor.
- EXPLAINER SCENES (infoClips I1..I3): copy DATA.planning.concept.scenePlan[].explainerScene when present — same sceneType, same subjectIds, same colors, same actions in the same order; declare every explainer object in subjects[] with the planned appearance as its traits (lowercase id, 1–12 chars). Each infoClip has id, stage, cleanPrompt (the objects in their starting state inside the explainer world, following explainerAnchor: no text, arrows, name tags or rings, with empty space around them), infoPrompt (the same scene after the actions with the emphasis devices, plus the infographic layer in words: which bold arrow, dimension line or ring points at which object and where each label sits), infoLines (1–4 short Korean lines, each at most 24 characters: exactly the text printed in the INFO image — numbers and facts copied from FACTS only, with Korean units, for example 올리브유 100% · 스페인산 · 600밀리그램; never an invented number, never English, never a sentence; the app reads the uploaded image and rejects it when the text differs), plan (CLIP PLAN describing the camera start, target, spatial route and final INFO viewpoint; no numeric time ranges inside camera or action text), sceneType, objects[{subjectId, color}], actions[] and emphasis[{kind, target, afterAction}]. graphicOrder, explanation and label fields do not exist in this policy. A comparison needs two or more objects in different colors and a color_code emphasis; its actions start with the models identical from the outside, then reveal the inside (cutaway, translucent shell or cross-section), and the color_code emphasis follows the reveal; the product's own object carries the same marker (accent1 or outline) in every scene; the same subjectId keeps one color in every scene; an emphasis target is one of that scene's objects and afterAction is smaller than the number of actions.
  Scene examples are relations, not reusable product actions: reveal a supported internal difference in a matched comparison; follow a documented material path in a process; distinguish a container boundary from the contents when a stated percentage applies only to the contents. A stated origin may connect the identified location with its material without inventing a shipping route. Use only facts supplied for this product and the existing sceneType/objects/actions fields; do not invent ingredients, empty comparison products or physical equivalences.
- CUTS: an explainer cut shows exactly one phase (early, mid or late) of its clip, lasts 2–{{EXPLAINER_CUT_MAX_SEC}} seconds (longer is rejected) and ends at most {{EXPLAINER_HOLD_SEC}} second after its last action; never a full 8-second hold. A comparison scene is used by two consecutive cuts: first its early phase (the models still look identical — rejected when the first cut of a comparison is any other phase) and then its mid phase (the inside revealed); a process scene is read in phase order early → mid → late. Live cuts stay within {{CUT_MAX_SEC}} seconds. Cut when the visual information changes. Live beats prefer veo_clip (hands turning, placing, reading, holding the product, with the person's face inside the frame) with 2–4 live clips (A..D) per video — the {{VEO_MAX_PERCENT}}% Veo share limit of the VEO section does not apply in this policy (Flow clips are paid per clip, not per second), so choose the phases needed for each live sentence rather than filling a footage quota and keep still_image cuts to at most 6 seconds in total; the first cut is a visible facial reaction plus one action within 1.5 seconds; still_image only establishes a place or holds a reaction, at most 3 stills in total and never two stills in a row under real_cause or requirement. Never describe a cut as postponed or on hold.
- TEXT: the only text inside an explainer frame is its infoLines, drawn as the infographic layer and revealed as complete labels at the relevant target/action in the shared plan; the app draws one caption line (the spoken phrase) below it and draws no callouts over explainer cuts. onScreenText on an explainer cut is that caption phrase. Live frames carry no generated text.
- CALLOUTS: callouts only on sentences whose cuts include live action; a sentence that plays only over explainer cuts has callouts [] (the explainer world carries its infographic labels and one caption line, nothing else). targetId is always null in this policy, and actionSync is always null (explainer scenes have no explanation beats; a sentence's cuts and their phases carry the timing).
- ENDING: the last 2–3 seconds are live action with the product (the person's hand holding it, or approved_image for at most {{HYBRID_ENDING_IMAGE_MAX_SEC}} seconds). Never end on a text panel or on an explainer scene.

## HYBRID_REVIEW_RULES

1c. HYBRID CONTRACT (policy hybrid_explainer_v1; check DATA.pairs, DATA.subjects, DATA.infoClips, DATA.styleAnchor, DATA.explainerAnchor and DATA.planning.concept.scenePlan): revise when (a) a pain, believed-cause, outcome or CTA sentence plays over an explainer cut or a text panel instead of live action that shows a person in a concrete situation; (b) an explainer scene's infoLines are missing, more than four, longer than 24 characters, in English, a full sentence, or carry a number or claim that is not in FACTS, or its infoPrompt puts the labels in a floating panel, table or checklist instead of attaching arrows, dimension lines and rings to the objects; (c) a comparison is not two models side by side under the same camera told apart by color; (d) the ending is not live action with the product (the person's hand holding it, or the approved image for at most {{HYBRID_ENDING_IMAGE_MAX_SEC}} seconds); (e) styleAnchor or a live prompt bans people, products or photographs, or explainerAnchor lets people or unplanned text in; (f) a planned explainer scene (scenePlan[].explainerScene) lost or changed its objects, colors or actions in the storyboard; (g) an explainer cut lingers after understanding is complete or cuts away before its planned relation and label can be understood; (h) a comparison whose first cut does not show the two models identical before the inside is revealed, or whose product marker changes between scenes; (i) a real_cause or requirement sentence carried by two stills in a row or by a still longer than {{STILL_BEAT_MAX_SEC}} seconds; (j) a live opening without a visible facial reaction and action, a pain scene with the face out of frame, or a phone or monitor with readable screen content. Also check R1–R9: different claims must not reuse an irrelevant action with only the label changed; the camera route, graphics sequence, single phase timetable and final INFO state must agree. Added graphic contrast colors and a readable settled viewpoint are allowed. Review the plan only; this is not verification of generated video frames. Point to the sentence and cut indexes.

## HYBRID_INFO_RULE

INFO CLIPS (explainer scenes): the real_cause, requirement, product_fact and reason_why steps may use explainer-world cuts instead of live action, at most {{INFO_CLIPS_MAX}} per video, declared in infoClips I1..I3 and used by cuts with source veo_clip, veoClip I1..I3 and ONE phase each (2–{{EXPLAINER_CUT_MAX_SEC}} seconds). Each infoClip has id, stage, cleanPrompt, infoPrompt, infoLines (the exact Korean labels and numbers printed in the INFO image, from FACTS only), plan, sceneType, objects, actions and emphasis (HYBRID PRODUCTION CONTRACT → EXPLAINER SCENES). graphicOrder, explanation and label fields do not exist in this policy. Use infoClips [] when the story has no explanation step; pain, situation, outcome and the next action stay live action (veo_clip A..D or stills).

## HYBRID_CLEAN_BASE_CLAUSE

## HYBRID_STRUCTURE_NOTE

; graphic scenes do not exist in this policy

## HYBRID_CALLOUTS_NOTE

In this policy callouts belong only to sentences with live-action cuts; a sentence that plays only over explainer cuts (I1..I3) has callouts [] and targetId is always null.

## HYBRID_SOURCES_APPROVED_IMAGE

Its use is optional: the ending may instead be the person's hand holding the product; when used it stays within the last {{HYBRID_ENDING_IMAGE_MAX_SEC}} seconds. Never insert an ad card just to satisfy a source quota.

## HYBRID_CUT_PHASE_NOTE

An explainer cut (I1..I3) uses exactly one phase, lasts 2–{{EXPLAINER_CUT_MAX_SEC}} seconds and is never a full 8-second hold; do not assign a second cut to the same phase of the same clip.

## HYBRID_GRAPHICS_RULE

GRAPHICS: motion_graphic is not available in this policy (rejected): a number, a list or a question is spoken and shown as onScreenText over live action or an explainer scene. graphicKind "" and graphicLines [] on every cut. Never invent an overlay field: photographic emphasis uses onScreenText.
